## `00. / ABOUT`

I’m a student developer drawn to the space where **technology, logic, and visual communication** meet.

My interest in development goes beyond making a page render or a feature work. I like understanding the structure beneath the interface: how a system is organized, how its parts communicate, why a particular solution makes sense, and how small decisions shape the experience of the person using it.

I approach digital work as a process of investigation. I break a problem into smaller pieces, explore possible solutions, test ideas, and refine the result. Sometimes that means thinking through the logic first; sometimes it means reconsidering the interface, the flow, or the way information is presented. The goal is not only to make something function, but to make it **clear, coherent, and intentional**.

My interests span programming, mathematics, electricity, and graphic design. They may look like different worlds, but each rewards curiosity, precision, and an understanding of how individual elements form a larger system.

### I. Programming — building systems

Programming is a way to turn an idea into a set of precise, executable decisions. I’m interested in how interfaces, application logic, and underlying structures fit together to create useful digital experiences.

- **Problem decomposition** — turning a broad problem into understandable parts.
- **System thinking** — considering how components interact rather than treating them in isolation.
- **Implementation and iteration** — building, testing, revisiting, and improving.

### II. Mathematics — reasoning with structure

Mathematics develops the habit of looking for relationships, constraints, and patterns. It offers a foundation for approaching technical problems with care instead of relying on guesswork.

- **Logic** — following an argument from its assumptions to its conclusion.
- **Abstraction** — recognizing a general structure behind individual examples.
- **Precision** — making reasoning explicit, consistent, and testable.

### III. Electricity — understanding what powers a system

Electricity represents an interest in the physical principles behind the technology we use. Circuits, energy, and the relationships between components are reminders that every system has an underlying structure, whether it is visible or not.

- **Principles before shortcuts** — understanding why a system behaves as it does.
- **Connections** — seeing how individual components contribute to a whole.
- **Practical curiosity** — learning by asking questions and investigating mechanisms.

### IV. Graphic design — making information communicate

Design is not decoration added after the work is finished. It influences how people interpret information, understand hierarchy, navigate an interface, and recognize what matters.

- **Visual hierarchy** — giving the right information the right emphasis.
- **Composition** — balancing elements so the whole feels deliberate.
- **Clarity** — using visual decisions to make an experience easier to understand.

> Different disciplines. A shared instinct: understand the parts, recognize the relationships, and shape the whole.
